Executive Summary

Buy the Martha Stewart French Door Air Fryer Toaster Oven if you want a large, attractive countertop appliance that can replace a basic toaster oven and handle family-size baking, roasting, air frying, reheating, proofing, dehydrating, and rotisserie cooking.

The pitch is simple: one handsome oven instead of several smaller appliances.

Compare first if crisp air-fryer performance is your main priority, your counter is shallow, or you want a small basket that heats almost instantly. Early hands-on evidence is encouraging for baking, toasting, usability, and capacity, but the ownership record is still young and the large format creates real counter-space and cleaning tradeoffs.

Skip If

This is not the right appliance simply because it looks better than a standard toaster oven.

  • Your main goal is the fastest, crispiest air frying for one or two people.
  • You have limited counter depth or cannot leave a large appliance permanently displayed.
  • You dislike cleaning racks, trays, a basket, crumb tray, and a larger oven cavity.
  • You expect a mature repair, parts, and long-term owner record from a newly launched appliance line.

Price Reality Check

The launch price is about $300, with recent promotional pricing around $200. At full price, it sits in premium countertop-oven territory; on sale, it competes more directly with large Ninja, Cuisinart, Emeril Lagasse, and Our Place models.

At this price, the 34-quart capacity has to replace real oven use—not merely toast bread.

The value is strongest when it reduces use of a full-size oven, replaces two countertop appliances, or gives a busy household a practical second cooking zone. Buyers who mostly reheat leftovers and cook single portions can spend far less.

Deep Dive

Large Capacity Is the Main Feature

The 34-quart cavity is designed for six slices of toast, a 12-inch pizza, family-size portions, or a whole chicken on the included rotisserie spit. That changes the product from a snack appliance into a legitimate secondary oven.

The buyer benefit is parallel cooking: dinner can continue even when the main oven is occupied.

  • French doors reduce the forward swing of one large door.
  • The cavity supports baking and roasting jobs that smaller air fryers cannot.
  • The rotisserie adds a genuine use case rather than another digital preset.

Buyer takeaway: Buy for usable capacity first; treat the long mode list as a bonus.

Twelve Modes Sound Better Than They All Matter

Air fry, toast, bake, roast, broil, reheat, slow cook, proof, dehydrate, cookie, pizza, and bagel settings cover an unusually broad range. Early reviewers found the controls intuitive and the preheat speed competitive.

Buyer takeaway: The proofing, baking, and large-cavity functions are more distinctive than presets that mainly change time and temperature.

Early Cooking Evidence Is Positive but Limited

A hands-on Kitchn review praised fast preheating, intuitive controls, toast-darkness selection, baking, and everyday versatility. Good Housekeeping also reported even cake baking. Those are useful signals, but they do not yet establish long-term temperature consistency, fan durability, door alignment, or coating wear.

Buyer takeaway: The evidence supports a compare-first recommendation, not blind confidence at full price.

Real-World Ownership Check

Large countertop ovens win on versatility and lose on physical presence. Measure before ordering and assume the appliance will remain on the counter.

  • Setup: Plan clearance around the sides, rear, and top, and confirm a suitable dedicated countertop outlet.
  • Maintenance: Expect regular crumb-tray cleaning plus washing of the rack, baking tray, air-fry basket, and rotisserie parts.
  • Fit: The 34-quart cavity means a substantial exterior footprint; cabinet clearance matters.
  • Workflow: One early reviewer noted the air-fry basket rests on another rack or tray rather than sliding independently.
  • Deal breaker: The interior light is not as controllable as some competitors, making mid-cook inspection less convenient.

What I Would Compare Next
