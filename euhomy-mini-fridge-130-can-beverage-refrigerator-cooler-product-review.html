Executive Summary

Buy it if you want a dedicated drink fridge without paying built-in-appliance prices. The EUHOMY 130-can beverage refrigerator combines a 3.2-cubic-foot cabinet, glass door, digital temperature control, removable shelves, reversible door, compressor cooling, and a listed temperature range down to 32°F in a package recently selling around $190 to $200.

The value proposition is simple: a lot of cold-drink storage for roughly two hundred dollars.

The strongest signal is demand. Amazon-market tracking estimated roughly 4,000 July sales for ASIN B0GCNN5MYR, while sampled listing data showed a 4.3-star average across more than 4,000 ratings. That is enough ownership volume to take the product seriously. The main caution is that beverage-fridge capacity claims assume tightly packed standard cans, and older professional testing of a closely related EUHOMY 3.2-cubic-foot beverage refrigerator found slower cooling and temperature readings that could run colder than the set point.

Skip If

The EUHOMY makes sense as a dedicated beverage appliance, but that is also its limitation.

  • You need a true food refrigerator with a freezer compartment rather than a drink-focused cooler.
  • You expect the advertised 130-can figure while also storing tall bottles, wine bottles, or mixed container sizes; real capacity falls as soon as shelf spacing becomes less efficient.
  • You need a built-in or under-counter refrigerator designed for enclosed cabinetry and front ventilation.
  • You want independently verified temperature precision for delicate wine storage; this is a single-zone beverage cooler, not a serious wine cellar.
  • You plan to place it outdoors or in an uncontrolled garage without checking the manufacturer's placement guidance and ambient-temperature limits.

Price Reality Check

Recent Amazon price tracking showed the black B0GCNN5MYR model moving from about $230 in June to roughly $189–$200 in late July. EUHOMY's closely related Fizz 110 sells direct for about $220 when available.

Around $190–$200 is the attractive zone; above $250, comparison shopping becomes more important.

At the lower price, the feature set is competitive: compressor cooling, digital controls, glass door, interior lighting, reversible door, frost-free operation, removable shelves, and a 3.2-cubic-foot cabinet. At a higher price, the question becomes whether you should move toward a better-supported premium beverage refrigerator, a dual-zone wine-and-beverage model, or a conventional mini fridge with more flexible food storage.

Product Deep Dive

The 130-Can Number Is a Packing Test, Not a Normal Grocery Load

The cabinet is listed at 3.2 cubic feet and roughly 18.5 inches deep, 17.8 inches wide, and 30.9 inches tall. EUHOMY advertises storage for up to 130 standard cans using removable shelves.

Capacity is strongest when your drinks are uniform.

  • Standard 12-ounce cans make the advertised number plausible under optimized shelf spacing.
  • Tall energy-drink cans, glass bottles, champagne bottles, and food containers consume vertical space much faster.
  • Removable shelves matter because they let you trade headline capacity for a layout that fits what you actually drink.

Buyer takeaway: If your refrigerator will hold a mixed collection, judge it as a 3.2-cubic-foot cabinet first and a “130-can” appliance second.

32°F to 61°F Covers Cold Drinks Better Than Serious Wine Cellaring

The current listing data for B0GCNN5MYR describes digital temperature control with a range extending from about 32°F to 61°F. That is unusually broad for a budget beverage cooler and makes the product flexible for beer, soda, sparkling water, and chilled wine.

  • Lower settings suit very cold canned drinks.
  • Warmer settings can work for serving some wine styles, though long-term wine storage has different consistency requirements.
  • A glass-door beverage refrigerator will usually have more temperature variation than a premium insulated wine cellar.

Buyer takeaway: The wide set-point range is useful, but the number on the display should not be treated as laboratory-grade temperature verification.

Compressor Cooling Is the Right Technology for This Size

This is a compressor-based refrigerator rather than a thermoelectric desktop cooler. That matters because a 3.2-cubic-foot cabinet needs enough cooling power to recover after door openings and handle a large load of room-temperature cans.

  • Circulating-air cooling is intended to reduce hot spots across the cabinet.
  • Frost-free operation reduces manual defrost maintenance.
  • Published noise claims for the related Fizz 110 are around 36 dB, although room acoustics and compressor cycling affect perceived noise.

Buyer takeaway: Expect refrigerator-like compressor cycling, not total silence. For a bedroom, noise sensitivity matters more than a single advertised dB number.

Real-World Ownership Check

The ownership questions are more practical than technical: where it will sit, how you will arrange cans, and whether you give the refrigeration system a proper setup.

  • Setup: Let the refrigerator stand upright before first use if it was transported on its side; manufacturer guidance for related EUHOMY units recommends a settling period before power-up.
  • Ventilation: Treat it as a freestanding refrigerator and leave the clearances specified in the manual rather than enclosing it tightly like a front-vented built-in unit.
  • Shelving: Expect the neatest capacity with uniform cans. Mixed beverages require experimentation with shelf positions.
  • Door fit: A reversible door is useful in offices and bars where wall clearance determines which direction the refrigerator should open.
  • Energy: Marketplace specifications list annual energy consumption around 252 kWh, so this is a continuously running appliance rather than a negligible plug load.
  • Warranty: EUHOMY advertises one-year limited warranty coverage through official channels including Amazon, plus customer support.
